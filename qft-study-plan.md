# QFT Study Plan

Based on *Quantum Field Theory for the Gifted Amateur* by Tom Lancaster & Stephen J. Blundell.

- **Pace:** about 6–8 hours per week
- **Core duration:** about 8–9 months, plus optional electives
- **Rhythm:** chapters are short (about 10 pages each), so aim for roughly two chapters a week, including exercises

---

## Phase 0: Refresher (4–6 weeks)

The book assumes undergrad physics is fresh. Reviewing these topics first will save trouble later.

### Classical mechanics
- [ ] Lagrangians and the Euler–Lagrange equations
- [ ] Hamiltonians and Poisson brackets
- [ ] Noether's theorem

### Quantum mechanics
- [ ] Dirac notation
- [ ] Harmonic oscillator with ladder operators (know this cold)
- [ ] Heisenberg vs Schrödinger pictures
- [ ] Time-dependent perturbation theory
- [ ] Spin

### Special relativity
- [ ] Four-vectors
- [ ] Index notation: upper and lower indices, the metric, the summation convention
- [ ] Lorentz transformations

### Electromagnetism
- [ ] Maxwell's equations in tensor form (F^μν)
- [ ] Gauge freedom

### Maths
- [ ] Fourier transforms
- [ ] Dirac delta functions
- [ ] Gaussian integrals, including the matrix version
- [ ] Contour integration and residues (the book's Appendix B covers this)

**Resources:** Susskind's *Theoretical Minimum* books on classical mechanics, quantum mechanics, and special relativity and classical field theory. Griffiths' *Introduction to Quantum Mechanics* is useful for practice problems.

**Checkpoint**
- [ ] Derive the ladder-operator solution of the harmonic oscillator without notes
- [ ] Write Maxwell's equations as ∂_μ F^μν = J^ν

## Study habits

- **Derive, don't just read.** Keep a dedicated notebook, and rederive every key result with the book closed.
- **Do some exercises in every chapter**, even just two or three. Reading alone gives a false sense of understanding.
- **Keep a second source for when you get stuck.**
  - David Tong's free Cambridge QFT lecture notes cover the same core material in a different voice.
  - A. Zee's *Quantum Field Theory in a Nutshell* is good for intuition.
- **Expect to loop back.** It's normal to reach Part IV and realise you need to reread Part III. That's part of how this material gets learned.

## After the book

- Matthew Schwartz, *Quantum Field Theory and the Standard Model*
- Peskin & Schroeder, *An Introduction to Quantum Field Theory*
